# Task 549: Alerts Fire Into Nothing: No Alertmanager, and k3s False Positives

**Issue**: [#549](https://github.com/dan-petty/devops-cli/issues/549)
**Status**: Done
**Milestone**: v0.2.25
**Priority**: priority/p1-high
**Scope**: type/bug, scope/k8s

## Description
kube-prometheus-stack was removed in #734, and #738 brought back the Prometheus server and Grafana as separate charts. The stack took its Alertmanager, its bundled rules and Grafana's Alertmanager datasource with it, so the stack ran with `alertmanager.enabled: false` and no alert rules: nothing alerted, not even on a node going down. Some of the removed rules had been false: `KubeControllerManagerDown`, `KubeSchedulerDown` and `KubeProxyDown` fired because k3s runs those components inside its server process, where nothing scrapes them.

Owner decisions (2026-10-03): no notification channel is chosen yet, so Alertmanager ships a receiver that sends nowhere plus one webhook whose URL comes from a Secret that does not exist yet; planned maintenance uses Alertmanager silences; metrics and logs are to be kept 30 days. Prometheus already keeps 30 days. Loki's enforcement and volume sizing are #550, merged first (PR #1092), so this item only watches Loki's retention and routes #550's two volume alerts like its own.

## Key Changes
- **`k8s/monitoring/prometheus-values.yaml`**:
  - `alertmanager.enabled: true`, on `local-path`, with resources, and pod annotations so the server's kubernetes-pods job scrapes it. Since #1130 k8s-monitoring's annotation autodiscovery scrapes it through the same annotations.
  - Receivers: `null` (no integration) is the default; a child route sends `severity=~"critical|warning"` to `webhook`, whose `url_file` is `/etc/alertmanager-webhook/url`. That directory is the Secret `alertmanager-webhook` (key `url`), mounted optional and read-only, with no `subPath`.
  - Inhibitions: `NodeNotReady` hides `TargetDown` on the same `node`; a critical alert hides the warning of the same `alertname`, `node`, `gpu` and `mountpoint`.
  - `serverFiles.alerting_rules.yml`: this item's 23 rules in four groups, next to #550's `log-and-metric-retention` group (2 rules), 25 in all:
    - `homelab-nodes`: `NodeNotReady` (5m, critical), `TargetDown` (10m, warning), `NodeRebooted` (uptime under 30 minutes, warning), `K3sServiceNotActive` (5m, critical), `GpuPowerLimitServiceNotActive` (10m, critical).
    - `homelab-gpus`: `GpuTemperatureHigh` (over 85 °C for 10m, warning; 90 °C or more for 2m, critical), `GpuPowerOverSiteBudget` (sum over 1000 W for 5m, critical; the comment says the budget should be per circuit once the new power layout is known).
    - `homelab-storage`: `HostFilesystemSpaceLow` (under 10% free for 15m, warning; under 5% for 5m, critical), `PersistentVolumeSpaceLow` (kubelet volume stats under 10% for 15m; its comment says no claim on this cluster reports those stats yet), `PrometheusStorageNearClaim` (TSDB over 80% of the `prometheus-server` claim for 1h). The claim rule reads this server's TSDB series by `job="prometheus"` and divides by `max` of the claim size, so a second Prometheus or kube-state-metrics series cannot stop `on ()` from evaluating.
    - `monitoring-self`: `ClusterMetricsMissing`, `PrometheusConfigReloadFailed`, `PrometheusRuleEvaluationFailures`, `PrometheusNotConnectedToAlertmanager`, `PrometheusDroppingAlerts`, `PrometheusCompactionsFailing`, `PrometheusSizeLimitCutHistory`, `LokiRequestErrors`, `LokiRetentionNotRunning` (its comment says it has no series until #550's Loki migration, whose Loki upgrade turns compactor retention on and adds the scrape annotations, and fires afterwards only if retention stops), `AlertmanagerConfigReloadFailed`, `AlertmanagerNotificationsFailing` (only once the webhook Secret exists).
    - `log-and-metric-retention` (#550): `LogOrMetricVolumeDiskLow` and `PrometheusNearRetentionSizeCap`, kept as #550 wrote them except that the size-cap rule's TSDB and limit selectors now read this server's self-scrape (`job="prometheus"`), as `PrometheusStorageNearClaim` does. Its matching is one-to-one, so a second Prometheus could not break its evaluation; the pin keeps a second Prometheus from being held to this server's `server.retentionSize` in the alert. The group's comment no longer says it reaches no receiver. The comments say where the rules overlap: `HostFilesystemSpaceLow` and `LogOrMetricVolumeDiskLow` both watch 10% free on the root filesystem of the node holding the claims, the second naming the claim at risk; `PrometheusNearRetentionSizeCap` warns before the size cap deletes blocks, `PrometheusSizeLimitCutHistory` once it has, and `PrometheusStorageNearClaim` only when no cap holds the data under the claim.

## Trade-offs
- The local-path volumes are host directories. They report no kubelet volume stats (0 `kubelet_volume_stats_*` series on 2026-10-03) and do not enforce their claim's size, so `PersistentVolumeSpaceLow` stays silent for them; it fires only for a volume type that reports stats. `HostFilesystemSpaceLow` covers the filesystem the Prometheus and Loki volumes live on, as does #550's `LogOrMetricVolumeDiskLow`, which names the claim at risk, and `PrometheusStorageNearClaim` compares Prometheus's data with the size its 30 days are planned for. Loki exports no on-disk size, so it has no claim rule.
- Logs are kept to 30 days only once #550's Loki migration has run; until then Loki has never applied retention. Nothing alerts on that before the migration, because the Loki scrape `LokiRetentionNotRunning` reads arrives with the same upgrade. #550's README section says so.
- `NodeRebooted` fires on uptime under 30 minutes, not on `changes(node_boot_time_seconds[...])`: a node that stayed down longer than the window has no earlier boot time in it, and the shared-breaker outages lasted hours.
- `NodeRebooted`, `K3sServiceNotActive` and `GpuPowerLimitServiceNotActive` read `node_boot_time_seconds` and `node_systemd_unit_state`, which #1080 (merged before this item) adds to the host metrics allow list. Until its k8s-monitoring upgrade is applied, those rules have no series and stay silent.
- `TargetDown` names targets without a `node` label by their `instance`. For node-exporter that is the node, so one silence covers it; the kubernetes-pods job (Loki, Alertmanager, the collector) sets `node` itself. Since #1130 Alloy's annotation autodiscovery sets it for Alertmanager, and the Loki integration puts Loki's node in `instance`. For cluster-wide targets the `node` label holds an address that matches no node.
- Planned maintenance needs the drain as well as the silence: `ClusterMetricsMissing` has no `node` label, so a node silence does not cover it if the single Alloy metrics pod is left on a powered-off node. While the node holding Prometheus or Alertmanager (local-path, so they cannot move) is down, no alert is evaluated or sent.
- `GpuPowerOverSiteBudget` uses one site-wide budget of 1000 W: the GPUs drew 837 to 1061 W together in the 20 minutes before every series stopped at 08:00 UTC on 2026-10-02, the start of an outage of about five hours. Over the 7 days to 2026-10-03, total GPU power stayed over 1000 W for a full 5 minutes in 15 one-minute steps and peaked at 1301 W. It should become one budget per circuit once the new power layout is known.
- `GpuTemperatureHigh` at over 85 °C for 10 minutes would not have fired in those 7 days; the hottest GPU peaked at 86 °C, for no 10 minutes in a row.
- Until the webhook Secret exists, every critical or warning alert group logs a failed notification each `group_interval` (5m). `AlertmanagerNotificationsFailing` waits for the Secret (`kube_secret_info`) so that expected failure does not alert.
- Backtest over the same 7 days: a 10-minute `TargetDown` would have been firing in 667 five-minute steps. Most were CoreDNS's metrics endpoint, which was unscraped until 2026-10-02. The rest were one node's outage, whose `TargetDown` the `NodeNotReady` inhibition hides. No target is down now.
- The whole site sharing one circuit means a trip takes Prometheus and Alertmanager down with the nodes, so no alert leaves the cluster for that event. An external heartbeat is the owner's open question and not part of this item.
