# Task: Homelab Hosts Send Their Journals to Loki and Their Health Metrics to Prometheus (#1080)

**Issue**: [#1080](https://github.com/dan-petty/devops-cli/issues/1080)
**Status**: Done
**Milestone**: v0.2.25
**Priority**: priority/p2-medium
**Scope**: scope/k8s

## Description
On 2026-10-03 all four live nodes lost power together three times. Nothing in the monitoring stack showed the reboots or their cause, and the timeline was rebuilt from each host's local journal. Prometheus had no boot time, temperature, pressure, memory-error, NVMe or systemd series, and Loki held pod logs and Kubernetes events only.

The k8s-monitoring chart (4.5.2) already runs node-exporter and the `alloy-logs` DaemonSet on every node. This item turns on the chart's own node logs feature and widens its node-exporter allowlist, with no new agent and nothing installed on a host.

The owner's decision (2026-10-03) is 30 days of history for homelab logs and metrics. Prometheus already keeps 30 days by time with no size cap, so the new host series keep 30 days from the apply. Loki keeps journal lines for 30 days only once #550's compactor retention (`retention_period: 720h` with `compactor.retention_enabled`) is applied; until then Loki deletes nothing by age. The issue depends on #550 for this reason, and the apply below runs after #550's Loki change.

## Key Changes
- **`k8s/monitoring/k8s-monitoring-values.yaml`**:
  - `nodeLogs` runs on `alloy-logs`, which mounts `/var/log` through its `filesystem-log-reader` preset and runs as root, so it reads the journal files (mode 0640). The relabel rules (`extraDiscoveryRules`) keep kernel lines at every priority, the `k3s`, `k3s-agent`, `nvidia-power-limit`, `containerd` and `systemd-journald` units at info and above, systemd's own lines about those units (journal field `UNIT`) at info and above, and every other line at warning and above. `journalLabels` adds `transport`, `structuredMetadata` adds `boot_id`, and the chart sets `job="integrations/kubernetes/journal"`, `instance` (the node name), `unit` and `level`.
  - `extraLogProcessingStages` adds a `stage.limit` per `unit` label: 1 line/s after a burst of 10,000, dropping the excess. Kernel lines carry no `unit` label, so they are never limited.
  - `hostMetrics.linuxHosts.metricsTuning.includeMetrics` adds `node_boot_time_seconds`, `node_hwmon_temp_celsius`, `node_hwmon_chip_names`, `node_systemd_unit_state`, `node_pressure_.*`, `node_edac_correctable_errors_total`, `node_edac_uncorrectable_errors_total`, `node_nvme_info`, `node_filesystem_avail_bytes` and `node_filesystem_size_bytes`. The last two were already kept by the chart's `node_filesystem.*`; they are named so the list holds without it. Since #1129 host metrics have no allow list; node-exporter serves these series and Prometheus keeps them all.
  - `telemetryServices.node-exporter` turns on the systemd collector with `--collector.systemd.unit-include` set to the same five units, and sets `DBUS_SYSTEM_BUS_ADDRESS` to the host's socket under the `/host/root` mount the chart already has. Helm replaces a list, so the chart's own `--collector.filesystem.fs-types-exclude` argument is repeated unchanged (the rendered DaemonSet diff shows only the two systemd arguments and the variable added).
- **`tests/test_k8s_monitoring_integration.py`**: `test_node_journals_reach_loki_through_the_alloy_logs_daemonset` pins the feature, its collector, the labels, the four keep rules built from one unit tuple, and the rate limit. `test_host_metrics_keep_the_series_that_explain_a_host_failure` pins the allowlist, the node-exporter arguments built from the same tuple, and the D-Bus address. Both failed on the previous values (`KeyError: 'nodeLogs'`, `KeyError: 'extraArgs'`) and pass now.
- **`k8s/README.md`**: a "Host Journals and Health Metrics" section, after the dashboards section, with what is kept, the labels, where Alloy counts the journal lines it drops, and four queries.

## Measurements (2026-10-03, read-only)
- **Collectors**: node-exporter 1.12.1 in the chart enables `stat` (boot time), `hwmon`, `pressure`, `edac`, `nvme` and `filesystem` by default and leaves `systemd` off (`node_exporter --help` in the running pod). Before the change, Prometheus held only `node_filesystem_avail_bytes`, `node_filesystem_size_bytes` (9 series each) and `node_time_seconds` of the requested names.
- **Systemd collector over D-Bus**: a transient node-exporter in two node-exporter pods, as uid 65534 with `DBUS_SYSTEM_BUS_ADDRESS=unix:path=/host/root/run/dbus/system_bus_socket` and the unit filter above, returned `node_scrape_collector_success{collector="systemd"} 1` and 15 `node_systemd_unit_state` series per node (3 units × 5 states). No live node runs `containerd.service`, since k3s embeds containerd, so that entry matches nothing today.
- **Series budget**: 100 new series per node. Counted from each node's exporter output for the new names, plus the 15 systemd series: 41, 47, 53 and 66 per node, 207 in all. Prometheus held 63,864 head series at 874 samples/s, with 464 MB of blocks for 3.1 days, about 2 bytes a sample. The new series add 3.5 samples/s, about 18 MB over 30 days on the 20Gi volume. Prometheus keeps 30 days by time (`prometheus_tsdb_retention_limit_seconds` 2,592,000) and has no size limit (`prometheus_tsdb_retention_limit_bytes` 0).
- **Journal budget**: 20 MB of text a day across the cluster (at most about 106 MB on disk over 30 days, under Retention below). The four nodes' journals for 2026-10-01 and 2026-10-02, read with `journalctl` under the same filter, keep 6.7 MB a day (7.5 MB on the busiest day, about 38,000 lines). Kernel lines are about 40% of it, mostly the firewall's rate-limited `[UFW BLOCK]` lines. Loki took in 1.27 GB and 3.0 million lines on 2026-10-02 (`query_loki_stats`), so the journals add about 0.6%. The busiest unit, k3s on the server node, peaked at 1,091 lines in a minute and 2,887 in an hour, inside the rate limit. gzip -1 compresses the kept text 10 to 18 times.
- **Retention**: Prometheus keeps 30 days by time (`prometheus_tsdb_retention_limit_seconds` 2,592,000) with no size cap (`prometheus_tsdb_retention_limit_bytes` 0), both re-read live. Loki deletes nothing by age yet: its live `/config`, read through the API-server proxy, has `retention_enabled: false` and `delete_request_store: ""`, and `loki_compactor_apply_retention_last_successful_run_timestamp_seconds` is 0, although `k8s/logging/loki-values.yaml` sets `retention_period: 720h` (it has no `compactor` block; #550 adds it). Loki's chunks compress 5.7 times (`loki_ingester_chunk_compression_ratio` sum 2,641.5 over 466 chunks), so the journals' 6.7 MB of text a day is about 1.2 MB a day on disk, 36 MB over 30 days, and the 20 MB a day budget at most about 106 MB. For #550's volume sizing, in the same unit: Loki wrote 45.3 MB of chunks (`loki_ingester_chunk_stored_bytes_total`) in the 11.6 hours since it started, about 94 MB a day or 2.8 GB over 30 days of pod logs and events against its 2Gi local-path volume, and the journals add about 1.3% to that.
- **Drop counters**: in Alloy v1.19.2 the journal reader counts every entry the keep rules drop as `loki_source_journal_target_parsing_errors_total{error="empty_labels"}` (`internal/component/loki/source/journal/tailer.go`, when `relabel.ProcessBuilder` returns false), and the rendered chart hands the rules to `loki.source.journal` as `relabel_rules`. `stage.limit` with `by_label_name` counts each line it drops in `loki_process_dropped_lines_by_label_total{label_name="unit", label_value="<unit>"}` (`internal/component/loki/process/stages/limit.go`). Prometheus holds no Alloy series (`count by (job) ({__name__=~"loki_source_.*|loki_write_.*|alloy_build_info|loki_process_dropped_lines_total|loki_process_dropped_lines_by_label_total"})` returns no data), so both counters are read on each `alloy-logs` pod's `/metrics` on port 12345, which answers through the API-server proxy.
- **Journal format**: the hosts' journal files use the ZSTD, keyed-hash and compact flags, all of which libsystemd 255 in the Alloy v1.19.2 image (Ubuntu 24.04) reads. Alloy loads libsystemd at run time for `loki.source.journal`.

## Trade-offs
- A unit's lines past 1 line/s, once its burst of 10,000 is spent, are dropped, not delayed: a unit stuck in a log loop shows a sample of the loop and the rest of the journal keeps flowing. Kernel lines are never limited, since the kernel and the firewall's logging rate-limit themselves.
- What the filter and the limit throw away is counted only on Alloy's own `/metrics`, which this stack does not scrape. The journal reader counts every entry the keep rules drop as `loki_source_journal_target_parsing_errors_total{error="empty_labels"}`, so that series counts the filter at work, not errors, and #549's alert rules should not fire on it. Lines the limit cuts appear per unit in `loki_process_dropped_lines_by_label_total{label_name="unit"}`, read with the week-after check above. Both reset when an `alloy-logs` pod restarts.
- Services that log to stdout reach the journal at info priority whatever their message says, so "warning and above" passes only lines sent with a real priority (systemd, the kernel, syslog callers). The issue's plan is to revisit the unit list and priorities after a week of volume data.
- After a restart, Alloy reads back the chart's default 8 hours of journal, since its read position lives in the container. Loki rejects the read-back lines more than an hour older than a stream's newest line as too far behind (an HTTP 400 in the Alloy log) and does not return the rest twice.
- Hosts in the inventory's offline group run no node and send nothing until they rejoin the cluster.
